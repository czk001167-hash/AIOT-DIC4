% ABSTRACT [A-F]

\begin{abstract}
% [A] Attention Getter
Conventional fixed-time traffic signal controllers are pre-configured from historical averages and cannot adapt dynamically to directional imbalances, temporal demand shifts, and sudden traffic surges.
% [B] But (Challenge)
Meanwhile, many modern adaptive signal control approaches rely on deep reinforcement learning, large language models (LLMs), or network-wide simulation frameworks that are too computationally heavy and opaque for constrained edge hardware.
% [C] Cure
This paper presents the \textit{Traffic Timing Assistant}, an explainable Domain AI Agent designed for two-axis through-traffic control at an isolated intersection using an ARM/FPGA Artificial Intelligence of Things (AIoT) co-execution architecture.
% [D] Development
The system integrates data sufficiency validation, recent-arrival and queue-state estimation, constrained timing-plan selection via a compact decision tree across three discrete modes (\textit{Balanced}, \textit{North--South Priority}, and \textit{East--West Priority}), deterministic rule-based fallback for out-of-domain states, cycle-boundary actuation on the FPGA, and hardware readback verification.
% [E] Experiments
Under identical synthetic arrival inputs, the proposed policy is benchmarked against a fixed-balanced baseline using Queue Vehicle-Seconds (QVS), average waiting time, remaining unserved vehicles, directional disparity, and hardware execution consistency.
% [F] Findings
Preliminary paired host simulations achieve total QVS reductions of 18.28\% across 18 scenarios (with 5 degraded cases) and 19.71\% across 6 scenarios (with 2 degraded cases), with degraded cases concentrated in balanced and sudden-burst demand regimes, while development-board tests confirm cycle-boundary plan activation and queue reduction in a directional-shift scenario. Because traffic performance is evaluated under simplified first-in, first-out (FIFO) queue models, real-world road benefits require separate field validation.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
Adaptive traffic signal control, Artificial Intelligence of Things (AIoT), explainable decision tree, ARM/FPGA co-design, rule-based fallback, hardware readback verification.
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}

% [G] Current State
Urban intersections rely on traffic signal controllers to allocate shared green time across conflicting movement axes. Traditional fixed-time signal plans are tuned from average traffic profiles and struggle when demand becomes directionally skewed, shifts across periods, or experiences sudden surges. Real-world municipal pilots and empirical traffic studies demonstrate the potential of demand-responsive signal timing: a Hong Kong adaptive signal pilot reported improved responsiveness to dynamic vehicle and pedestrian demand~\cite{ref_hk}, while big-data adaptive timing analyses across 100 cities indicated potential reductions in urban congestion and emissions~\cite{ref_wu}.

% [H] Challenges
Despite these encouraging results, two practical challenges hinder lightweight edge deployment and instructional or engineering validation:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textit{Explainability and Execution Auditability:} Displaying raw traffic counts or toggling signal lamps alone fails to answer \textit{why} a specific timing plan was selected or \textit{whether} the embedded hardware actually applied the recommended plan.
    \item \textit{Compute and Safety Constraints:} Recent AI signal controllers frequently rely on large spatio-temporal networks or LLM-based reasoning~\cite{ref_collmlight, ref_vtp}, exceeding small-board resource budgets and lacking deterministic phase-safety guarantees.
\end{enumerate}

% [I] Literature Categories & [J] Literature Critique
Prior research spans both AI control architectures---such as policy distillation into inspectable decision trees~\cite{ref_viper} and multi-agent or hierarchical LLM controllers~\cite{ref_collmlight, ref_vtp}---and traffic domain studies covering adaptive deployments~\cite{ref_hk, ref_wu} and signal timing engineering standards~\cite{ref_fhwa}. However, heavy graph and LLM models target large-scale simulation environments rather than transparent, resource-bounded embedded development boards.

% [K] Core Contributions & Scope
To address this gap, we propose the \textit{Traffic Timing Assistant}, a scoped Domain AI Agent targeting two-axis through-traffic at an isolated intersection. Designed for classroom demonstrators and embedded AIoT developers, the system establishes end-to-end traceability from input data to hardware actuation around three core objectives:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Demand Suitability:} Benchmark a compact decision tree augmented with rule-based fallback against a fixed-balanced timing baseline under identical arrival profiles.
    \item \textbf{Decision Explainability:} Trace every timing choice to observed arrival/queue features, decision-tree split paths, and explicit out-of-domain fallback reasons.
    \item \textbf{Execution Verification:} Close the hardware loop by reading back the active timing plan, signal phase, and countdown state from the ARM/FPGA platform.
\end{itemize}
The prototype scope is deliberately bounded to a single intersection with three discrete timing plans (\textit{Balanced}, \textit{North--South Priority}, and \textit{East--West Priority}) executed across an ARM processor and an FPGA phase controller. Multi-intersection coordination, pedestrian signal phases, turning movements, and live roadside deployment are outside the current scope.

% [L] Paper Organization (Addresses Proposal Gap L)
The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section~II reviews related work across AI policy extraction and traffic signal engineering, and defines our research gap positioning. Section~III presents the proposed design, synthesizing domain design rationales, the eight-module ARM/FPGA architecture, processing workflow, core algorithm, and queue formulation. Section~IV details the experimental setup, preliminary host and board-level results, and validity limits. Finally, Section~V concludes the paper and outlines future work.

\section{Proposed Design}

\subsection{Domain Design Rationale and Trade-offs}
% [PA-PF] Core Mindsets (M1-M5) and Engineering Trade-offs (C1-C3)
Drawing from~\cite{ref_hk, ref_wu, ref_collmlight, ref_vtp, ref_viper, ref_fhwa}, five core mindsets and three architectural trade-offs shape our design:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{M1 --- Demand-Driven Formulation:} Because adaptive gains vary across demand regimes~\cite{ref_hk, ref_wu}, scenarios span balanced, skewed, and shifting demand alongside a fixed-time baseline.
    \item \textbf{M2 --- Decoupled Decision and Execution:} Following hierarchical control~\cite{ref_vtp}, the ARM host executes traffic analytics and plan selection while the FPGA enforces safe phase sequencing and countdown timers.
    \item \textbf{M3 --- Teacher-Rule Approximation Awareness:} Inspired by VIPER~\cite{ref_viper}, the decision tree learns from domain scoring rules; approximation errors are acknowledged rather than assuming the tree outperforms teacher rules.
    \item \textbf{M4 --- Evidence-Scoped Claims:} Per simulation-vs.-pilot distinctions~\cite{ref_hk, ref_collmlight, ref_vtp}, data diagnostics, host FIFO simulations, and board-level execution are evaluated separately.
    \item \textbf{M5 --- Retention of Negative Counterexamples:} Per multi-objective signal trade-offs~\cite{ref_fhwa}, aggregate average gains can hide localized directional penalties; both improved and degraded cases are retained.
\end{enumerate}

Three explicit engineering trade-offs resolve key design tensions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{C1 --- Rules vs.\ Decision Tree:} Handcrafted rules offer direct traceability, whereas a decision tree demonstrates supervised policy learning and compact board inference~\cite{ref_viper}. We generate training labels from domain scoring rules and deploy the compact tree with rule-based fallback for out-of-domain inputs.
    \item \textbf{C2 --- Immediate vs.\ Cycle-Boundary Switching:} Mid-cycle switching reacts faster to bursts but risks phase disruption and countdown inconsistency~\cite{ref_fhwa}. We commit new plans strictly at complete cycle boundaries on the FPGA~\cite{ref_vtp}.
    \item \textbf{C3 --- Mean Delay vs.\ Operational Completeness:} Mean delay reduction~\cite{ref_wu} must be weighed against directional fairness and safety limits~\cite{ref_fhwa}. We report directional metrics, unserved vehicle counts, and degraded cases alongside overall QVS.
\end{itemize}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Module 1 --- User \& Structured Request} \textit{(Integration Target)}: Specifies the traffic data source and mode (closed-loop actuation or review-only recommendation).
    \item \textbf{Module 2 --- Data Source \& Validation} \textit{(Integration Target)}: Verifies source identity, timestamps, unit consistency, and history sufficiency; halts control and reports gaps when data are insufficient.
    \item \textbf{Module 3 --- Analysis Engine} \textit{(Core Ready)}: Extracts recent arrivals and estimated queue lengths for both North--South (N--S) and East--West (E--W) axes.
    \item \textbf{Module 4 --- Decision Tree Classifier} \textit{(Core Ready)}: Maps the four axis features (N--S arrivals, E--W arrivals, N--S queue, E--W queue) to \textit{Balanced}, \textit{N--S Priority}, or \textit{E--W Priority}.
    \item \textbf{Module 5 --- Domain Constraints \& Fallback} \textit{(Core Ready)}: Enforces legal plans, minimum/maximum green durations, clearance intervals, and out-of-domain fallback rules.
    \item \textbf{Module 6 --- Decision Rationale \& Reporting} \textit{(Integration Target)}: Logs feature values, decision-tree split paths or fallback triggers, and comparative outcomes.
    \item \textbf{Module 7 --- ARM/FPGA Execution} \textit{(Core Ready)}: Transmits the validated plan from ARM to FPGA and latches it at the next complete cycle boundary.
    \item \textbf{Module 8 --- Readback \& Display} \textit{(Core Ready)}: Reads back the applied plan, active phase, and countdown timer from hardware, supplying \textit{execution evidence} to Module~6 and \textit{state feedback} to Module~3.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Processing Workflow and Core Algorithm}
% [PH-PI] Flowchart (Fig. 2) and 12-line Pseudocode (Algorithm 1)
Fig.~\ref{fig:flow} illustrates the runtime decision and verification flow. At each observation step, the controller verifies data sufficiency before updating queue and arrival estimates. At designated decision boundaries, in-domain features invoke the compact decision tree whereas out-of-domain features trigger deterministic rule fallback. Before hardware actuation, the system logs decision rationale and checks review-only mode and plan/device validity. Validated plans are applied by the FPGA at the next complete cycle boundary and verified via readback, as formalized in Algorithm~\ref{alg:control_loop}.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Integrated Timing Decision and Verification Loop}
\label{alg:control_loop}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\STATE Read task mode and traffic data
\IF{data is insufficient}
    \STATE Report the data gap and \textbf{stop}
\ENDIF
\FOR{each observation}
    \STATE Update queues and recent arrivals
    \IF{not at a decision boundary}
        \STATE \textbf{continue}
    \ENDIF
    \IF{features are in-domain}
        \STATE Select plan with the decision tree
    \ELSE
        \STATE Select plan with fallback rules
    \ENDIF
    \STATE Record actual features, decision path, and fallback reason
    \IF{review-only mode}
        \STATE Report recommendation and \textbf{continue}
    \ENDIF
    \IF{plan or device state is invalid}
        \STATE Report diagnostic status and \textbf{continue}
    \ENDIF
    \STATE Submit plan; apply it at the next complete cycle
    \STATE Read back the applied plan; report success or mismatch
\ENDFOR
\STATE Summarize decisions, execution results, and limitations
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Separating data sufficiency (Line~2), domain applicability (Line~10), and hardware readback (Lines~21--22) provides end-to-end auditability from raw input to physical signal actuation.

\subsection{Case Design and Queue Formulation}
% [PJ-PM] Case Representation, Data Design, and Mathematical Queue Conservation
Constraining the output space to three discrete timing plans (\textit{Balanced}, \textit{N--S Priority}, \textit{E--W Priority}) over four numerical demand features simplifies both tree interpretability and hardware state verification. Training labels are generated from a domain timing-scoring rule, with phase boundaries bounded by configured minimum green, maximum green, and clearance constraints in Module~5. Synthetic arrival profiles enable reproducible second-by-second paired evaluation, whereas official minute-level traffic records are assessed separately for continuity and data quality rather than used directly as per-second simulation inputs.

Under discrete-time first-in, first-out (FIFO) queue conservation over horizon $T$ seconds, the per-axis queue evolves at each second $t$ as:
\begin{equation}
q_d(t+1) = \max\bigl(0,\, q_d(t) + a_d(t) - s_d(t)\bigr), \quad d \in \{\text{NS}, \text{EW}\},
\label{eq:queue_update}
\end{equation}
where $a_d(t)$ denotes vehicle arrivals and $s_d(t)$ denotes served departures permitted by the active FPGA signal phase and intersection service capacity. Cumulative \textit{Queue Vehicle-Seconds} (QVS) over horizon $T$ is then defined as:
\begin{equation}
\text{QVS} = \sum_{t=1}^{T} \left( q_{\text{NS}}(t) + q_{\text{EW}}(t) \right).
\label{eq:qvs}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Validity Scope and Limitations}
% [V] Validity Limitations
All traffic efficiency metrics in Table~\ref{tab:prelim_results} originate from synthetic inputs and the simplified FIFO queue model~\eqref{eq:queue_update} rather than live roadside trials. Hardware execution verification and simulated traffic efficiency are evaluated independently. Integration work on unified request entry, cross-source validation, and unified rationale reporting (Modules~1, 2, and~6 in Fig.~\ref{fig:arch}) is ongoing. Moreover, available official minute-level traffic records lack the continuous, dual-axis per-second resolution required for closed-loop intersection control simulation and are therefore restricted to data-quality diagnostics.

\section{Conclusion}

% [W] Restate Research Significance (Addresses Proposal Gap W-Z)
This paper presented the \textit{Traffic Timing Assistant}, an explainable edge AIoT system that unifies input data validation, interpretable signal timing selection, and verifiable ARM/FPGA hardware actuation for an isolated two-axis intersection.

% [X] Methodological Distinction
Rather than deploying opaque, resource-intensive deep reinforcement learning or LLM agents, the proposed design pairs a compact decision tree over four arrival/queue features with deterministic out-of-domain rule fallback and cycle-boundary FPGA phase switching.

% [Y] Deliverables and Verified Outcomes
The prototype establishes an auditable decision-to-readback pipeline validated through paired baseline benchmarks, demonstrating total QVS reductions of 18.28\% across 18 host scenarios (with 5 degraded cases) and 19.71\% across 6 host scenarios (with 2 degraded cases), alongside verified cycle-boundary plan activation and queue improvement on the development board under directional shift.

% [Z] Limitations and Future Work
Several limitations define concrete directions for future work:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Degraded-Case Ablation:} Conduct systematic parameter sweeps and root-cause analyses across the degraded cases observed in both host test batches (5 of 18 and 2 of 6)---particularly balanced and sudden-burst demand regimes---to refine decision thresholds and cycle-switching hysteresis.
    \item \textbf{High-Resolution Empirical Data:} Incorporate continuous per-second roadside sensor logs to replace simplified FIFO synthetic assumptions and validate real-world arrival dynamics.
    \item \textbf{Extended Intersection Modeling:} Expand control capabilities to pedestrian clearance phases, protected/permitted turning movements, sensor noise robustness, and multi-intersection coordination.
\end{itemize}
